Move from “AI-powered system” to **“what do I get and what can I do with it?”**

From abstract intelligence to:
**“See everything. Know what changed. Take the right action.”**

---

### Core Principle (User Language First)

Every page must speak like the user thinks.

* Not: “AI detects churn risk signals”

* But: “See which accounts are at risk — before they churn”

* Not: “multi-source data orchestration”

* But: “All your customer data, in one place”

* Not: “cognitive overlay”

* But: “Daily brief of what changed and what needs attention”

Focus on:
**What the user sees → What it means → What they can do**

---

### Feature-Led Communication (Not AI-Led)

Lead with **features and outcomes**, not technology.

Users are buying:

* Visibility
* Control
* Speed
* Confidence

Not:

* AI models
* Pipelines
* Architecture

---

### This Must Apply Across All Pages

Every page must follow:
**What you get → What you see → What you can do**

But each page must express it differently.

---

### Do Not Mirror Pages

* No repeating the same section patterns
* No cloning homepage structure across pages
* No generic “feature grids” everywhere

Each page must feel:

* Different in layout
* Different in flow
* Different in emphasis

---

### Page-Level Direction (User Language)

**Platform (Spine) Page**

* Focus: “Everything connected”
* Speak in outcomes:

  * “See data from Salesforce, Stripe, Slack in one view”
  * “No more switching tabs”
* Show simple flows, not architecture diagrams

---

**Workbench (Product) Page**

* Focus: “What you see daily”
* Show:

  * Account timeline
  * Health score
  * Morning brief
* Language:

  * “Open one account → see everything → decide fast”

---

**Solutions (by Role) Pages**

* Focus: “Your job, simplified”
* Example:

  * Customer Success:

    * “Know which accounts need attention today”
    * “Track renewals, risks, and conversations in one place”
* Speak their workflow, not your system

---

**Governance / Trust Page**

* Focus: “You stay in control”
* Language:

  * “Nothing changes without your approval”
  * “Review before anything is sent or updated”
* Show approval moments, not AI logic

---

**Pricing Page**

* Focus: “What you get for the money”
* Keep it simple:

  * “Access to all integrations”
  * “Role-based workspaces”
  * “Approval workflows included”
* No technical jargon

---

### Experience Goal

The user should feel:

* “I can finally see everything”
* “I know what to do”
* “I am in control”

Not:

* “This is a powerful AI platform”

---

### Final Rule

If a sentence sounds like it’s written for engineers, rewrite it.

If it talks about AI before value, remove it.

If the user cannot immediately understand what they gain, simplify it.

Every page must answer:
**“What do I get, and how does it help me today?”**
